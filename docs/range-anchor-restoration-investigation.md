# Restoring a published range at an exceptional anchor

This is an investigation for the next recurring-scheduling batch, not an approved protocol or implementation contract. [ADR 0172](adr/0172-durable-published-range-controls.md) rejects a range-cancellation anchor that already has a published single-occurrence override or individual cancellation. That guard remains in force.

The difficulty is preserving two different published effects at one original occurrence: the anchor may have its own timing, while later originals follow the surrounding series or a future-timing segment. A restoration that copies the anchor's exceptional timing into a `THISANDFUTURE` component could unintentionally change subsequent occurrences. The current native override validator also rejects a single and future anchor with the same original identity. The supported scheduling wire profile must be checked independently of what a remote calendar client happens to display.

Consider a daily series at 09:00 whose Tuesday original was moved to 11:00, while Wednesday and later remain at 09:00. Cancelling Tuesday and all future occurrences must suppress both. A faithful restoration must recover Tuesday at 11:00 and Wednesday onward at 09:00, preserve any independently cancelled originals, and leave newer native drafts untouched. A solution must also handle a final occurrence, later range changes, PERIOD additions, DST gaps/folds, and bounded work without expanding an unbounded series indefinitely.

One candidate is a single-occurrence projection at the anchor plus a future projection at the next valid original, when one exists. This is only a candidate: it requires proof that all affected original identities and durations retain their published meanings, that collisions with existing future components can be represented, and that version/recipient handling remains valid. A finite series with no next original needs its own defined result. An individually cancelled anchor needs separate analysis because restoring the suffix must not restore that individual's cancellation. If the projection cannot be proved faithful within existing limits, it must reject rather than produce a partial message.

Next, establish the relevant RFC and current parser/export constraints and produce concrete examples for the above cases. Then review a bounded pure projection and its test vectors before designing durable checkpoints, source authorization, retries, recipient behavior or UI. Exact external-client behavior requires separate interoperability evidence; no real email is authorized by this investigation.

## Current code evidence

`eventSchedulingRangeRestoration` validates the anchor's membership, then rejects an existing single or retained single identity at that anchor. It separately rejects a PERIOD anchor when no future segment is active. Later PERIOD originals are materialized as finite single projections and the union remains capped at 64 originals, with membership checks charged to the shared recurrence budget. `validateEventOverrides` rejects duplicate single/future identities. These are current application guards, not assertions that every such schedule is impossible under RFC 5545 or RFC 5546.

A feasibility experiment must distinguish a next original from the next chronological displayed start: a moved exception can cross the cutoff, and recurrence exclusions or DST-generated gaps can remove nominal candidates. It must check collision with an existing future component at that next original and prove behavior when there is no next original before the supported horizon. The experiment should have no persistence, authorization, generated endpoint, worker or transport effects; any runtime change needs a separately accepted ADR.
